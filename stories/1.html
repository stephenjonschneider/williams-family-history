LONG ISLAND · 1640s–1660s

A Dutch bride and an English town

The oldest thread in this history is Penelope, the wife of Richard Stout. New Jersey tradition says she was a young Dutch woman, born Van Princis, who survived a shipwreck and an attack near Sandy Hook, was cared for by the Lenape, and reached New Amsterdam, where she married Richard. The records cannot test that story, and her birthplace and her famous age of 110 remain tradition. What they do show is solid: she sold land beside Richard in 1679/80 and acknowledged the deed in person, and his will of 1703 calls her “my loving wife.” Together with Richard, she is remembered as the “Mother of Middletown.”

The Stouts made their home at Gravesend, the English town founded by Lady Moody on the Dutch side of Long Island. Their son Richard was born there about 1646. Their daughter Mary was the first link between two founding families.
