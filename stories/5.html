MIDDLETOWN TO FREEHOLD · 1667–1761

The Laytons

William Layton came to Middletown by way of Portsmouth, Rhode Island, with his wife Violet. In 1667 he drew town lot No. 14, and by 1670 he was a paying townsman of the Monmouth purchase. He was also one of the Baptists, alongside Richard Stout, James Grover and the Bownes, who founded the Middletown Baptist Church, organized in 1688 and the oldest Baptist congregation in New Jersey. He named five sons in his will of 1702. The Laytons and the Bownes were neighbours from the start: William Bowne's 1677 house-lot survey names Layton next door, and a Middletown meadow ran between John Bowne's land and William Layton's. When the next generation moved inland to Freehold, the neighbours came too. William Layton Jr's 1690 tract on Pesequenecque Brook had Richard Stout Jr on its north line, Samuel Layton's 1715 land right came down from Obadiah Bowne, and by 1717 a Stout tract lay between Samuel Layton and Thomas Williams. The Bownes bought there too: John Bowne, a Middletown merchant, took 100 acres on Passequenecka at the Great Meadow in 1698 and 200 acres on the Burlington Path in 1701, and held 750 acres at Wickatunk until 1709.

There John Layton, a yeoman of Freehold and later Shrewsbury, married Catherine. She was a Bowne by tradition, and the case that she was Samuel and Esther's daughter is now strong: no record names her parents, but her children's names repeat their household, and both of the other placements have failed. Through her came Stout, Bowne and Grover blood into the Laytons. They named a son Safety. John's will of 1761 names Safety, Andrew, John, William and Thomas. Which Layton was John's own father is not yet settled. The evidence now leans to the settler's son Andrew: in 1800 Andrew's son, another Andrew, left money to “the heirs of my brothers James, Samuel and John Layton.” Either way, every road leads back to William Layton the settler.
