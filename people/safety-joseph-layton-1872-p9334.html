What the records say

Safety Joseph Layton was born in Brooklyn on 29 November 1872 (his WWI draft card and Social Security application of December 1936), the son of Safety Layton, a carpenter born in New Jersey, and Emma Nosworthy, born in England [S-0192; S-0210]. He appears with his parents and sister Lillie E. in 1875 and 1880 [S-0202; S-0199]. In 1900, while his father was at the Bath soldiers' home, he was an unmarried express driver living on Bushwick Avenue with his mother and sister [S-0210]; in 1905 he headed the household at Cornelia Street as a painter [S-0211]. In 1915 he was a painter living with his mother, then 72, and sister Lily, a department-store saleslady, at Chauncey Street [S-0213]; at his draft registration on 10 September 1918 he was 45, a painter for Waterbury & Reilly of Murray Street, Manhattan, short, of medium build, blue-eyed and grey-haired, his nearest relative his mother Emma [S-0214]. He died on 15 June 1942 and was buried at Cypress Hills, where his father lies [S-0212].
