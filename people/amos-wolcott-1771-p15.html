What the records say

Amos Wolcott first appears as a witness to his father's executors' deeds and his brothers' sales of 1792 and 1796 (WO-0020, WO-0021, WO-0022). In 1792 he signed among the bride's family, with his mother Clementine (G9) and his brothers, at his sister Meribah's Quaker wedding (WO-0050). Tax lists show him a householder in 1794 and on a house and lot to 1808 (WO-0114). In 1800 he and his wife Lydia (G8) sold part of 'the lot whereon Benjamin Wolcott dwelt', their share under his father's will (WO-0024), and in 1803, described as a carpenter, he mortgaged a lot beside James Lafetra's (WO-0045). A family compilation says he married Lydia Borden in 1794 outside the Quaker meeting, was disowned for it, and died about 1830, and lists his children, among them Henry H. (G7) and Amos Jr (WO-0075); a county biography names the same brothers for Henry H. (WO-0081). A family tree's death before 1817 is contradicted by the 1808 tax list.
