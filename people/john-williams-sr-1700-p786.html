What the records say

In September 1730 Thomas Williams (G11) sold land to "John Williams, carpenter" (W-0001), and in 1737 sold him the Crosswicks tracts (W-0012). From 1733 John kept the county jail at Lower Freehold (W-0085). On 25 and 26 September 1746 he took administration of his father Thomas's estate as his son and of his brother Joseph's (G10) as his brother (W-0062, W-0061). By 1761 he had a sawmill on the Metedeconk (W-0296), and in 1771 he and Joseph Throckmorton took a mortgage on land at the head of "John Williams's saw mill pond" (W-0146). He borrowed heavily in New York money, and a debt of 1758 led to judgments against his lands (W-0123). A 1780 deed calls a plantation southwest of Freehold courthouse the one "John Williams formerly lived on" (W-0196). A Loyalist, he conveyed his Metedeconk and Dover lands to his housekeeper, the widow Jean Lewis, on Long Island on 11 May 1781, and died there during the war (W-0133, W-0065). His brother Daniel's 1789 will left his Stafford lands to John's three daughters (W-0063).
