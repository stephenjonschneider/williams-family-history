What the records say

John's earliest record is an indictment: in 1778 'John Williams, son of Joseph Williams', of Shrewsbury, was charged with breaking into the Lower Freehold house of John Longstreet, Esquire, on 1 April 1777 with up to three others and taking a quantity of bacon (W-0455). The Shrewsbury Township tax lists of 1779 and February 1780 again call him "John Williams, son of Joseph" — a householder with no land, one horse and one cow, valued at £2 5s (RT-117, RT-126). The same lists carry a second John, "son of John", so the clerks used fathers' names to tell the two apart. He is gone from the October 1781 list. In 1789 a John Williams was sued for debt by Kenneth Hankinson, the man who sold Daniel Williams (G8) his Miery Branch land (W-0131, W-0021), but no record shows that he is the same John. No record names his wife or children.
