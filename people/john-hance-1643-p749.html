What the records say

John Hance came to Monmouth from Dover, New Hampshire, where the 1657 tax list names him next to Thomas Hanson; he probably married Thomas's daughter Elizabeth [WO-0289–WO-0291]. He bought the whole Monmouth Patent share of John Coggeshall in 1668 [WO-0216]. In 1679 the Proprietors laid out his home, a strip running across Rumson Neck from the Shrewsbury River to the Navesink, called in 1688 and 1695 the land where he lived [WO-0217–WO-0222]. With his brother-in-law Tobias Hanson he was one of the 1685 Manasquan purchasers, and he held land there and at Barnegat [WO-0205, WO-0220, WO-0223]. He sat as a Monmouth justice, and in 1699 he sold land as attorney for 'his nephew' Tobias Hanson, the heir of the purchaser killed at Dover in 1693 [WO-0225, WO-0290]. His will of 1708 names his daughter Elizabeth Worthly, an ancestor of Lydia Borden (G8) [WO-0189].
