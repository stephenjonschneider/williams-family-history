NAVESINK · 1664–1670

The men who bought Monmouth

In the early 1660s, Gravesend and Rhode Island men looked across the bay to the Navesink country. In December 1663 a Dutch yacht sent along the shore recorded an English boat bound for the Raritan and the Navesink, and named three men in it: “Jan Bon,” John Bowne; Richard Stout; and “Jems” Grover. Governor Richard Nicolls's patent of 8 April 1665 names twelve patentees. Three of them belong to this family: Richard Stout, James Grover and Captain John Bowne, James's brother. One account counts Richard and Penelope Stout's household among the first five families to settle on the purchase in 1664.

James Grover was more than a name on the list. He had a Gravesend house lot by 1646, raised the English flag there in 1655, and in 1668 carried the Monmouth settlers' letter to Governor Nicolls. When the purchase accounts were settled in 1670, the payments from every purchaser went to “James Grover & Company.” With John Tilton and William Goulding, he had put up the money and goods that paid the Lenape sachems for Monmouth. The Bownes were among the first on the ground: James had lot No. 2 at Portland Point by 1667 and became a patentee in 1669. Stillwell records a family tradition that James Bowne's eldest child, by his wife Mary Stout, was the first child born in Middletown. Shares in the patent changed hands early. In 1668 John Coggeshall of Rhode Island sold his whole share, at Navesink, Rumson, Portapeck and the Gift Land, to John Hance of Shrewsbury, an ancestor of the Wolcott line through his daughter Elizabeth.
