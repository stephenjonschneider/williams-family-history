What the records say

Thomas Cooper witnessed two Middletown releases, in 1722 beside Benjamin Cooper's land and in 1724 at the mouth of Claypit Creek (C-0113, C-0114). In March 1732 John and Thomas Davis gave "Thomas Cooper of Middletown, mariner" a £300 bond to give him land on the north side of Claypit Creek (C-0311). On 30 May 1744 his father Benjamin Cooper deeded him land at Shoal Harbour, and on 7 July 1753, as a yeoman of Lower Freehold, he sold two acres of it to James Davis (C-0312, C-0313). Abel Tilton sued a Thomas Cooper for work in 1751 (C-0120). He was dead by 17 May 1755, when his executors - his widow Phebe, his son Thomas and John Williams of Freehold - sued Albert and William Covenhoven (C-0022). The compiler's tree makes him the father of Ellenor Neeltje (G9) (C-0031); an online memorial gives him 1706–1745, which conflicts with the 1753 deed (C-0333). A Thomas Cooper appears among the Middletown names on the 1748 list of Monmouth freeholders (C-0410).
