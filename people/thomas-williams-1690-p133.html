What the records say

Thomas Williams witnessed a Freehold deed in February 1710/11 (W-0018), and in January 1714/15 he bought James Stout's Freehold homestead for £250 (W-0007). With the consent of his wife Mary he sold Freehold land in 1720 and 1729 (W-0009, W-0010), and in April 1730 he bought two tracts near Crosswicks from Abraham Lincon (W-0011). That September he sold a Freehold tract to John Williams, carpenter (W-0001). In 1724 he appraised an estate and witnessed Lawrence van Hook's will (W-0249, W-0250), and by 1733 he was "of Upper Freehold" (W-0074). In 1737 he sold the Crosswicks tracts to John Williams (W-0012). He died without a will; on 25 September 1746 his son John took administration of his estate (W-0062), and the next day John administered the estate of his brother Joseph (G10) (W-0061).
