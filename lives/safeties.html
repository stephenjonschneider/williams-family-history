MONMOUTH AND BEYOND · c.1658 – 1976

Safety – All of Them

Every man named Safety the research has found, and what they tell us about Catherine Layton

Safety is one of the rarest first names in early America. It belongs to the old Puritan habit of naming children for a virtue or a prayer, like Sorry-for-Sin or Free-gift, which was common in Sussex parishes before 1650. James Grover, one of the men who bought Monmouth, gave it to his youngest son about 1658. The surprising thing is not the name itself but that it never died out. The idea to test is simple: every Safety, in Monmouth and wherever its families went, descends from James Grover. If that is true, then a Layton named Safety must have had a Grover-descended parent, and that points straight at Catherine, the wife of John Layton. To test it, the research went looking for every Safety it could find. It found twenty-five, plus one who turned out never to have existed.

The first. Captain Safety Grover was born at Gravesend about 1658. A warrant of 1680 lays out land for “Safety Grover the sonn of James,” and he lived at Middletown into the 1740s as militia captain, justice and landowner; he was still alive after October 1745. Every other Safety comes down from his family, and almost always through a daughter. And every Safety named for him, from Safety Borden in 1682 to Safety Layton about 1737, was named while he was still living: the name honored a living grandfather through his daughters' families.

The Bordens and the Meghees. Safety's sister Abigail Grover married Benjamin Borden, and their son Safety Borden, born in 1682, appears in his brother James's papers as “my Trusty & Loving Brother.” He married a daughter of Captain Safety, his own cousin, and settled near Crosswicks and later at Bordentown. In his will of 1757 he left land to “my grandson, Safty Meghee.” That grandson, Safety Meghee, son of Amy Borden and William Maghee of Allentown, was a Bordentown cordwainer and tanner and one of the founders of the Upper Freehold Baptist church in 1766. His own will of 1770 names five children, Amy, William, Mary, Safety and Sarah. William died an ensign of the 5th Pennsylvania in 1777. Safety Meghee Jr married Rachel Cadwallader at Abington, Pennsylvania, in 1790 and lived to nearly a hundred.

West to Ohio and Illinois. Amy Meghee married Thomas White, a tanner who took the family to Ohio, and named her youngest son Safety McGee White; he died a baby. Her daughter Mary White married James Henderson, and they named a son Safety McGee Henderson. He was born near White Hall, Illinois, in 1826 and farmed there until his death in 1908. This one is special: the test predicted that a Safety McGee Henderson would turn out to be a Meghee descendant before the records proved it, and they did. His brother James White Henderson named a son for him: a second Safety McGee Henderson, aged 2 in his father's Macoupin County household in 1850, who married Elvira Howard in Iowa in 1873 and died a farmer at Southwick, Idaho, in 1916. Two more men, an uncle and nephew both named Safety McGee Hone, lived in Hocking County, Ohio: Safety McGee Hone (9 June 1824 – 22 April 1886) and Safety McGee Hone (16 August 1846 – 23 December 1907). The full name Safety McGee appears nowhere except among Safety Meghee's descendants, so the Hones very probably belong to his line too. Their father Henry Hone was born in 1795 to Peter Hone and his wife Mary of Washington County, Pennsylvania, so the name most likely came in through Mary, whose maiden name is not recorded. Safety Meghee's daughter Mary is the leading candidate. She was baptized at the Upper Freehold Baptist church in 1780 and was still single when the Southampton Baptist church in Bucks County, Pennsylvania, received her in December 1784; after that she leaves no record under her own name. Until a record ties the two Marys together, this stays a working idea.

Grover daughters at home. Captain Safety's daughter Hannah married William Taylor, and two separate Taylor family accounts list their son Safety Taylor, whose gravestone records his death in November 1766. William Taylor's will of 1767 leaves him out only because he had died nine months earlier. Another daughter, Esther, married Samuel Bowne, and their son Safety Bowne appears in a family arbitration of 1767 as “son of Samuel Bowne, dec'd.” The two sisters' families match: each named a son Safety and daughters Catherine and Esther, and Catherine Layton later repeats the same three names.

The Laytons. When John Layton made his will in 1761 he named his eldest son Safety, born about 1737. From him the name spread through the Laytons. His youngest son Safety, a wheelwright of Howell at Polly Pod Bog, died in 1840. A Safety, son of John, died in 1841, and Safety Layton Jr took up land in 1833. A Safety Layton bought the Marsh homestead at Woodbridge in 1815, and a Safty Layton and his wife Mary were in Salem County by 1830. One branch went to Brooklyn. Safety Layton, son of John S. Layton, born at Farmingdale in February 1842, became a carpenter, fought with the 133rd New York Infantry and died at the soldiers' home at Bath in 1914; his son Safety Joseph Layton lived in Brooklyn until 1942. Another Safty, son of Albert Layton of Salem and Delaware, rode the Methodist circuits of the Delmarva peninsula and Indiana, served as chaplain of the 9th Indiana Infantry in 1861–62, and ended as a Chicago lawyer who also dealt in Missouri land. He died in 1882, and a little Safty M. Layton is buried in the Friends' cemetery at Spiceland, Indiana.

The last. The youngest Safety Layton's daughter Mary married Samuel Bennett in 1847 and died at Key East, Neptune Township, in 1889; her death certificate names her parents as “Safty Layton” and “Abby.” Her son Andrew, born at Neptune City, farmed at Allenwood, and his son Safety Bennett was born at Asbury Park on 23 September 1896. Safety Bennett, a 19-year-old farmer of Allenwood, married Luella Haley on Christmas Day 1914 at his sister's house in West Grove, Neptune Township. He died on 2 March 1976 at Lawrenceville, Mercer County, and is buried there. He is the last Safety the research has found. The name had run in this family for more than three hundred years, from Captain Safety Grover to Safety Bennett.

What all of them show. Of the twenty-five, every man whose family is known descends from James Grover, and not one turns up in an unrelated family. Eleven of them, the Bordens, Meghees, Taylor, Bowne, White, the two Hendersons and the Hones, are traced through Grover daughters without any help from Catherine Layton. The name almost always travels through daughters, and grandchildren revive it in full, as Safety McGee shows. Only two are still untested: a Safety Borden named as a grandson in a New Brunswick will of 1753, and Dr Safety S. Richards of Licking County, Ohio, born in 1851, whose father came from Maryland. If Dr Richards's mother turns out to have no Grover descent, he will be the first exception.

Why it matters for this family. The Layton Safetys cannot prove Catherine's parentage by themselves, since they are the very people the question is about. But the eleven independent cases show that the pattern holds, so the Safety in Catherine's family points to a Grover-descended mother. Samuel and Esther (Grover) Bowne are the couple who fit, and that is a large part of why Catherine's parentage is now graded Strong. The name is also one more thread in a pattern the whole app traces: the deeds, wills and surveys keep putting the Grovers, Bownes, Bordens and Laytons side by side for generations, just as Stillwell and the other early Monmouth genealogists found. One record could make it Documented: Samuel Bowne's estate distribution of 1756, if it names his daughter Catherine Layton.
