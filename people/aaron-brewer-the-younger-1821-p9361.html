What the records say

Aaron Brewer built a holding at the Blue Ball crossroads by Long Brook and the Methodist meeting house: half-acre lots from Joseph and Phebe Haviland and from David Cooper in 1821, 4.75 acres from David Richmond, owner of the grist mill (BR-0096), and the tavern lot from Tunis Forman in April 1824; he was called a miller in 1825 (BR-0037, BR-0038). From 1826 to 1830 he kept the Washington Tavern at Freehold (BR-0039, BR-0094), mortgaging steamboat shares and then "the Blue Ball property" to William J. Newbold in 1828 (BR-0076, BR-0104). With his wife Rachel he sold Shopsunk pine land in 1827 (BR-0082). With his wife Eleanor, born Eleanor Cooper, he sold Shopsunk woodland in 1835 (BR-0077) and joined her fellow legatees of John Cooper in dividing his estate in 1836 (BR-0100, BR-0101). With his wife Mary Ann, "of Blue Ball", he mortgaged the tavern lot and other land in 1847 and 1849 (BR-0087, BR-0088), and they sold lots in 1852-1864. In 1866 county sales and a lunacy inquisition were held at his house at Blue Ball (BR-0103, BR-0080).
