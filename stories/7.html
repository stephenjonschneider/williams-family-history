FLATLANDS TO ADELPHIA · 1657–1801

The Brewers

From the 1690s a wave of Dutch farm families from Kings County, Long Island (Flatbush, Flatlands, Gravesend), moved into Freehold and Middletown, and by the mid-1700s they were about a third of the county. The family's own deeds show it: a Suydam of Flatbush bought Thomas Williams's farm in 1729, and Van Cleves of Gravesend were Layton neighbours. The Brewers came with that wave. Jan Brouwer, a blacksmith from Amsterdam, settled at Flatlands, Long Island, after 1657. His descendant Jan Brouwer, baptized at Flatbush in 1695, was in Middletown by 1719 and married Helena Van Cleef, daughter of Isbrandt Van Cleef and Jannetje Vanderbilt. The family tree calls her Hilletje, the Dutch pet form of Helena, and the baptisms show the two names are very probably one woman. Their son Aaron Brewer, born Aris Brouwer about 1724, farmed between Long Brook and the Manasquan at Blue Ball, today's Adelphia. His wife Ellenor Neeltje Cooper was born at Middletown in 1726. Her family was very probably the Middletown Coopers: Benjamin Cooper had come over from Fresh Kills on Staten Island by 1712, and Thomas Cooper, given as her father, was very probably his son. Her Dutch first name hints that her mother's side came from the same Long Island world, and Cooper is itself one of the oldest names in Monmouth. Her 1785 gravestone is the earliest dated burial in the Adelphia churchyard, and in 1801 the Methodist burying ground was laid out on Brewer land. The sons of John Layton held their shares nearby on the Mill Brook and Long Brook. After Ellenor's death Aaron married Martha Stillwell in 1786. His will of 7 April 1800 names his daughter “Mary Williams,” and he left her £50. Another Brewer family farmed a few miles south on Mingamahone Brook, Elazarus Brewer and his sons, but DNA shows they were no kin: they descend from Adam Brouwer of Gowanus, a different Long Island family.
