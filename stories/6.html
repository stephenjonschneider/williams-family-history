CROSSWICKS & UPPER FREEHOLD · 1688–1746

West to the Crosswicks country

The families also moved west. In 1688 James Bowne took up 480 acres at Crosswicks. By 1708 John Stout held land at the east end of the neighbouring Throckmorton tract, by 1711 Abigail Grover's son Benjamin Borden Jr had a field there, and Bownes still held land there in the 1730s. In April 1688 seven Middletown men, among them James Bowne, Joseph Grover and Benjamin Borden, were each granted 500 acres “adjoining to the pines at Crosswicks”. Their lots lay side by side along Doctors Creek, and in 1690 John Barker's survey ran “along the rear of the Middletown men's lots”. Thomas Williams, a yeoman of Freehold from 1710, moved to Upper Freehold near Crosswicks and Doctors Creek by 1730, with 440 acres. His farm lay in that same block, beside Richard Borden's land. His son Joseph married Sarah Layton, daughter of Thomas Layton. This is the first Williams–Layton marriage on record. Father and son both died intestate in 1746. Thomas had already handed the Crosswicks estate to his son John in 1737. John later farmed 529 acres in Freehold and ran sawmills on the Metedeconk, but he sided with the Crown in the Revolution, went over to British-held Long Island, and died there during the war. His family's 3,000-plus acres in Monmouth were confiscated and resold.

The next step is a working hypothesis, not a proven one. A John Williams, listed as “son of Joseph” on the Shrewsbury tax lists of 1779 and 1780, may be Joseph's son and the father of the Daniel who comes next.
