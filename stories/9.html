BURLINGTON, SHARK RIVER & BURNT TAVERN · 1685–1882

Newmans and Wolcotts join

The Newmans lived along Shark River and Wreck Pond for generations. The line begins with John Newman of Shark River, whose 1768 will names his wife Deliverance and their sons, among them John, born about 1737. That John's son John (1762–1830) and his wife Rebecca appear in the family Bible kept by their son Jeremiah. Joseph I. Newman, named in his 1801 deed as “Joseph Newman, son of John Newman Junr,” began a farm on the Cross Branch of Wreck Pond that year and married Lydia. After Joseph I. died in 1846, his children shared the farm. His son Silas Newman was married in 1831 by John Saplin Newman, an elder of the Methodist Independent Church, and became a Methodist preacher himself. He gave the lot for the Harmony church in 1844, bought land on the North Branch in 1846, and died on 10 August 1859, in the 24th year of his ministry. He was buried at Harmony. His daughter Catherine Louisa married Daniel D. Williams at the Methodist parsonage in Farmingdale on 7 September 1862.

The Wolcotts reach back to Samuel Wolcott of Shrewsbury, whose will was proved in 1687. That same year Peter Woolcott, by then the Quaker meeting's gravedigger and keeper of the Friends' burying ground at Burlington, had a son Benjamin with his wife Jone. Peter died in 1690. He may have been Samuel's son, but that is not yet proven. Benjamin's family were Shrewsbury people again, settled around Eatontown. Henry H. Wolcott kept a store and tavern at Burnt Tavern. His son Amos, a jeweler, was the father of Mary A. “May Anne” Wolcott, born in 1865. On Christmas Eve, 24 December 1882, she married Thomas Edward Williams, Daniel D. and Catherine Louisa's son, near Freehold. In 1939 the couple marked their 57th anniversary on Christmas Eve.

Benjamin Wolcott, who kept looms and a weave shop and at times spoke in meeting, died on 6 April 1790. His son Amos, born in 1771, married Lydia Borden out of meeting in 1794, and through her the family reaches back into Shrewsbury's oldest Quaker households. Lydia's father John Borden was born in 1742 to John Borden and Elizabeth; her mother Lydia Worthley was a daughter of Richard Worthley and Mary White, who married at Mary's father's house in 1747. The Bordens go back through Francis Borden to Francis and Jane Borden, at Shrewsbury by 1677. The Worthleys go back to Elizabeth Hance, who married John Worthly at the Shrewsbury meeting house in 1696 with her parents signing the certificate. Her father, John Hance, had bought a Monmouth patent share in 1668 and lived on a strip across Rumson Neck, between the Shrewsbury and Navesink rivers. He sat as a Monmouth justice from 1676, and in 1685 he was one of the men who bought land on the Manasquan; his patent of 1687 gave him 250 acres there in five tracts.
