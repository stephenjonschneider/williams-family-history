What the records say

Benjamin Wolcott, a weaver of Monmouth, gave marriage bonds with Rachel Wainwright on 16 October 1749 and, as a weaver of Shrewsbury, with 'Cleffey' (Clementine) Cook on 27 February 1753 (WO-0159, WO-0126). Shrewsbury Friends took him under their care in 1755 (WO-0104); by 1761 he affirmed as a Quaker when proving a neighbour's will (WO-0162), and he served on meeting committees in 1765–68 (WO-0103, WO-0106). A patriot record credits him with paying the 1780 taxes and gives his birth as 19 July 1724 (WO-0107). His will of 31 July 1784 gives his wife 'Clemey' the house and lot, partly to support her father, leaves his looms and weave shop to 'all my Children and their mother', and divides his estate among his seven children Peter, Jacob, Benjamin, Elizabeth, Henry, Merebah and Amos (G8) (WO-0160). Friends reported that he, who 'at times had a few Words in Public', died on 6 April 1790, aged about 66 (WO-0214); the will was proved on 28 July 1791, and his executors, his son Peter and Timothy Corlies, settled the estate by 1801 (WO-0139–WO-0141).
