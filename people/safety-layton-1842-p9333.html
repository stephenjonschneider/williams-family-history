What the records say

Safety Layton was born at Farmingdale, New Jersey, in February 1842 (his muster card; 1900 census) [S-0205]. In 1860 he was an apprentice carpenter, 19, in the Brooklyn household of his father John Layton, carpenter, with his mother Eliza M. and brothers Garrett and John H. [S-0204]. He married Emma Nosworthy on 20 August 1862 [S-0197] and the next day enlisted in Company I, 133rd New York Infantry, serving to 6 June 1865. A Brooklyn carpenter with three children (Lilly E., George W., Safety Joseph) [S-0199], he was installed as Commander of Mansfield Post No. 35, Grand Army of the Republic, in January 1877 [S-0215], was listed as a carpenter at Ninth Street about 1880 [S-0216], served on the arrangements committee of the Mansfield Post No. 35, Grand Army of the Republic, ball in 1886 [S-0208] and lived at 353 South 4th Street in 1890 [S-0209]; he lived from about 1900 at the State Soldiers' Home at Bath, where he died on 17 September 1914 [S-0206]. He is buried at Cypress Hills, Brooklyn [S-0207].
