What the records say

On 28 Mar 1693 John Bowne of Middletown sold Thomas Layton, planter, of the same place 100 a. on Pesaquanogus Brook (L-0024, L-0062). His father's (G12) will of 1702 names him (L-0022, L-0413). In 1709 John Chamberlain sold Thomas Layton of Freehold, husbandman, more land on the same brook (L-0029), and in 1715 Gawen Drummond conveyed him a right near Cattail Meadow (L-0034); he sold his quarter of the Cattail Brook mill tract to John Bond in 1719 (L-0060). A Thomas Layton sued Francis Masters, cordwainer of Shrewsbury, in 1732 (L-0420). He died intestate in Lower Freehold by 14 Sept 1740; the inventory of £170, taken by John Williams and John and Thomas Layton, includes bonds of Christopher and Peter Romine and four slaves, and his daughters Sarah, wife of Joseph Williams, and Rachel, wife of Peter Romine, were his heirs (L-0048). Stillwell's date of 26 Sept 1741 is a year late.
