What the records say

Samuel Woolcott witnessed a Navesink land deed at Newport, Rhode Island, in December 1674 (WO-0005). In March 1677 a warrant laid out land at Shrewsbury in right of himself and his wife (WO-0006); a survey followed, and in January 1681/2 he received a patent as 'Samuel Woolcott of Shrewsbury, yeoman' for 150 acres on the south side of the Navesink, beside Stephen West and Colonel Morris (WO-0007, WO-0008, WO-0011). His will of 7 May 1687 named Judah Allen and Thomas Webley executors and was proved on 1 December 1687; it mentions his son Nathaniel, then serving Joseph Grover (WO-0087, WO-0147). In 1690–92 Nathaniel sold the land to Edward Williams (WO-0012, WO-0013). Later deeds and patents refer back to his right (WO-0016, WO-0017, WO-0018).
