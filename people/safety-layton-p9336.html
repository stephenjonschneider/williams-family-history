What the records say

With his brother-in-law Ephraim Marsh he bought up the shares of Christopher Marsh's heirs in the Marsh homestead at Woodbridge on 1 March 1814 - four heirs' quarter for $1,512.90 and the late John E. Marsh's share for $432.25 [S-0170, S-0172]. On 17 May 1815 Ephraim sold him the whole 60.41-acre homestead, on the road from Milton to Brunswick, with a salt meadow, for $2,536.25 [S-0176], and the next day he and Hetty mortgaged it back to Ephraim for $2,535, both acknowledged in August 1815 [S-0173]. On 2 November 1816 he bought from George Brown half of the grist and saw mills at Milton for $4,500 [S-0174], and on 1 October 1818, with Hetty, sold that half to Asher King for $3,250 [S-0168]. Ephraim Marsh's will of 1817 calls him "my brother in law Safety Layton" and Hetty "my sister" [S-0164]; in September 1818 he sued Ephraim's executors [S-0165].
